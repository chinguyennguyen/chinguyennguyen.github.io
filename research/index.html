research

My dissertation, "Social Spillovers in Political Participation," focuses on voting behavior and immigrant integration policies using Swedish administrative data. I combine econometric methods with machine learning to understand political participation patterns and evaluate policy effectiveness.

work-in-progress

Family Spillovers of Youth Voting

Using Swedish administrative voting records from the 2018 and 2022 elections and the age-18 election-day eligibility cutoff, I estimate spillovers from first-time voting within families. Youth voting increases turnout among parents by 1.5 percentage points and older siblings by 1.1 percentage points, with effects concentrated among geographically close relatives and parents with lower baseline turnout propensity. Effects on the young person’s own turnout persist, while family spillovers are concentrated around the first election.

Daughter Effect on Parental Voter Turnout

Using Swedish population registers and the quasi-random sex of first-born children, I study whether political engagement can flow upward from children to parents. Mothers of adult daughters are 0.7-0.8 percentage points more likely to vote than mothers of adult sons, with smaller effects among fathers. The effect is strongest among mothers with a foreign background, greater cultural distance from Swedish gender norms, and lower pre-birth household income shares, suggesting that adult daughters can act as a source of intergenerational political mobilization.

Where the Vote Counts: Moving and Electoral Participation with Patrik Reichert

We use a rule requiring domestic movers to relocate at least 30 days before an election to vote in their new municipality. Movers just after the cutoff are 1.4 percentage points more likely to vote in the parliamentary election but abstain from the municipal election, suggesting that participation responds to where a ballot has electoral influence.

Immigrant Voting Rights and Integration Outcomes

I use discontinuities in immigrants’ arrival dates that determine whether local voting rights are obtained after three or seven years of residence. Linking voting eligibility to subsequent labor-market, language, and residential outcomes, I find no evidence that earlier access to voting rights improves integration.

Neighborhood Effects on Refugee Integration with Cecilia Ahsan Jansson

We use the 2016 refugee allocation to apartments to estimate how initial neighborhood characteristics affect subsequent labor-market and language outcomes and residential choices.

Improving Voter Turnout Prediction

I build XGBoost models to predict how Sweden’s 7.4 million eligible voters transition between voting and non-voting across the 2018 and 2022 municipal elections, rather than modeling turnout in isolation. I test balanced and stratified sampling strategies at scales from 50,000 to 4 million observations, and examine geographic variation in predictability across Sweden’s 290 municipalities to identify which municipal characteristics drive differences in model accuracy.
